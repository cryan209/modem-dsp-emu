# BRI wire fidelity correction, 26 October 2026

The BRI live adapter now preserves the firmware's PCMU negative-zero
octet through the SIP PCM16/firmware-encoder boundary. This is a verified
byte-fidelity correction, not proof that the historical training failures
were caused by signed zero.

## Reproduction and correction

The BRI SPORT1 kernel already produces companded wire octets. The adapter
expands them to PCM16 for the SIP endpoint, which re-encodes them using the
resident firmware G.711 encoder. Both PCMU zero codes, 0x7f and 0xff,
expanded to integer zero; the encoder then returned 0xff for both. Testing
all octets reproduced exactly one PCMU mismatch, 0x7f -> 0xff; A-law had
none. The resident encoder maps PCM16 -1 to 0x7f and zero to 0xff.

`BriKernelModem.frame_fast()` now returns -1 for the already-companded
PCMU 0x7f code, retaining its sign through re-encoding. Other samples and
A-law are unchanged. No DSP state, firmware arithmetic, training decision,
gain setting or peer data is forced.

The regression feeds every octet through the adapter and the actual SIP
RTP queue/firmware encoder. It failed on PCMU 0x7f before the correction;
afterward all 256 PCMU and 256 PCMA octets match their original wire bytes.
The test stubs the ring output to isolate the boundary; existing real-kernel
SPORT tests separately cover the ring/ISR and companding behaviour.
